Current closeout: the bounded snapshot-client/Outcome integration is merged in [PR104](https://github.com/riventic/air2lean/pull/104); the general reusable synchronization contracts and independent-client scope above remain incomplete.

Follow-up (condition broadcast, coordinator decision on [PR599](https://github.com/riventic/air2lean/pull/599)): `CondImpl` has no `broadcast_wp` because no translated implementation provides broadcast yet, and a contract method no implementation proves is not admitted; a client that calls broadcast has no contract and is rejected. To close it: (1) translate a std condition broadcast in a fixture (`Io.Condition.broadcast` exists in 0.16.0 and 0.17.0, `std.Thread.Condition.broadcast` in 0.14.1 and 0.15.2); (2) add `broadcast_wp` to `CondImpl` on the existing generic `FCond.PostOk` / `FCond.SigPost` with `all = true`, proved by every implementation; (3) relax `FCond.Inv.wake`'s premise `n ≤ c` to `min n queue ≤ c`, since the OS broadcast wakes `waiters - signals`, which can exceed the epoch queue.

Follow-up (condition cancellation and timeout, [PR599](https://github.com/riventic/air2lean/pull/599) review): `CondImpl.wait` is the uncancelable wait. The cancelable `Io.Condition.wait` (0.16.0, 0.17.0) and `waitTimeout` (0.17.0) leave on cancel or timeout with a waiter's `waiters -= 1` (0.17.0 `deregister`: state `(w - 1, min s (w - 1))` and an `epoch += 1`). `FCond.prim` admits neither, since a state step is a registration, post or consume (`SStep`) and epoch writes happen only at a signaller's `inc → wk`. So a client calling them has no contract and is rejected. To close it, the primitive must admit a waiter's deregistration (a new `CPh` transition `reg → out` with its state write and, for 0.17.0, its epoch write), the invariant must count it, and a `wait_cancelable_wp` must be proved by every implementation.

Follow-up (the epoch window, coordinator decisions on [PR575](https://github.com/riventic/air2lean/pull/575)): `FCond.Fits.win` and `FSem.Fits.win` ask the protocol for fewer than `2^32` epoch writes after the save of every registered waiter on its way to its futex wait (`reg i jr false e`, neither queued at the epoch nor woken: the one step that uses it is the sleep), not of waiters already queued, woken or back from their wait (narrowed in [PR641](https://github.com/riventic/air2lean/pull/641), maintainer decision 2026-11-04). It is sound. A waiter that registers again under contention saves the newest epoch write at its load (engine-ledger row `cond-epoch-load-floor`, closed: [PR630](https://github.com/riventic/air2lean/pull/630) and the lock's word invariant at the primitive's step), when every epoch write happened before the mutex's holder (`FLock.WordHB`, `FCond.ld_newest`). So a client with at most one registered waiter at a time proves `win` when its rest bounds the epoch writes per registration. A semaphore client with one registered waiter at a time can prove this bound itself, SemCounter first, because `FSem.Go` admits `wt` only from `out`, and `pst` only from `out` or from `wt` at a count not 0 (engine-ledger row `sem-go-code-moves`, closed; the row has the counterexample for the unrestricted `Go`), and only a thread in the client's waiting role `FSem.wx` enters `wt` and the condition (engine-ledger row `sem-wait-role`, closed; the row has the counterexample). The waiter's take is indexed by the client parts at the take (engine-ledger row `sem-take-index`, closed). Still open, for two or more registered waiters (engine-ledger row `cond-window-multi-waiter`): a registered waiter that does not reach its futex wait keeps its saved write while the others go on. Counterexample: waiters A and B registered, a poster P; A is not scheduled after its registration (or it reloads the epoch in its wait loop, `reg i → reg i'` outside the mutex, keeping `i' = i`: the primitive keeps only `i ≤ i'`); P signals, B consumes the signal and registers again, and P signals again; after `2^32` rounds A is at `reg i`, not queued, with more than `i + 2^32` epoch writes. This is the ABA that std's comment in `Condition.signal` describes. A long-running client with two or more waiters under an unfair wake also cannot discharge it, because one waiter can stay queued while the others take more than `2^32` signals. The narrowed `win` closes the second case but not the first (A is not queued); an eventual-fairness premise does not give the bound either. So a long-running client with two or more registered waiters also needs a quantitative premise (a registered waiter reaches its futex wait before `2^32` further epoch writes) for the first case (maintainer decision). Its way into a proof is open (the ledger row): stated about the protocol's states it would be false in the model, so it must be an obligation on runs, which needs a model or `Proto` opt-in. One with fewer than `2^32` posts in all bounds the epoch writes by them. `SemImpl.post` asks for an unmetered fragment `> 0` on the kept part (the tick resource of [PR562](https://github.com/riventic/air2lean/pull/562)'s policy, as `MutexImpl.lock` and `CondImpl.signal`); a client that posts its whole part keeps none and cannot pay.
