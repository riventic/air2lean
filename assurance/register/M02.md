in-place success, relocated success and failure; specify preserved bytes, invalidated pointers and capacity changes. Acceptance: both successful growth paths and failure cleanup are proved and differentially tested with suitable allocators.

Bounded progress: [PR139](https://github.com/riventic/air2lean/pull/139): the translated `PageAllocator`'s `resize`/`remap` are proved from its code over the OS model: `mremap` grows in place when the grown pages are free, moves under `MAYMOVE` (copying the live bytes, ending the old mapping) or fails; a shrink unmaps the cut pages (`TotalTriple.mremapGrow`/`mremapShrink`, `TotalTriple.munmapTail`; a mutant that leaks them is rejected).

Native comparison of the proved paths (`tests/roadmap/alloc-translated`, [alloc-page.md](docs/alloc-page.md#growth-by-remap-and-realloc-m02)): `page.zig`'s `page_grow`, `page_grow_blocked` and `page_grow_stale` grow a block of the translated `PageAllocator` through `Allocator.remap` and `Allocator.realloc`. The model's lines (over the 0.16.0 and the 0.17.1 translation, with a main-thread stack reserve as natively) and the native run's lines (stock Zig 0.16.0 and 0.17.1, `ReleaseSafe`) are compared with one file per target: x86_64-linux natively (`mremap`: grown in place, moved when a live neighbour is in the way, failed for a `2^47`-byte length; the old address faults after the move) and aarch64-macos (no `mremap`: `remap` fails past the page with the old block unchanged, `realloc` copies to a new mapping). No mismatch.

Against the acceptance sentence, for Zig 0.16.0 and 0.17.1 on x86_64-linux and aarch64-macos: the in-place growth, the relocated growth and the failure cleanup are proved (`PageSpec.remap_spec`, `resize_spec`, `PageSpecMacos`: one proof text over both versions' page-allocator translations; the fixed buffer's `FBA.fallocSpec`, Zig 0.16.0 and 0.17.1) and compared with the native runs of the page allocator and the fixed buffer; the std-mode byte buffer has its own native probe (`tests/roadmap/resize-remap`). Left inside the sentence:

- Not forced natively: a kernel that moves a mapping although there is room in place. The model runs it (its oracle `mremapMoves`, proved in `remap_spec`); nothing in user space makes a kernel take that choice, so whether the proof and the model's run suffice for it is a maintainer decision.

Closed since: a growth `mremap` that fails for lack of memory (`ENOMEM`) is forced natively on Linux. A child process of `native.zig` reads its mapped page count from `/proc/self/statm`, sets `RLIMIT_AS` to one page more and calls `page_grow(10, 10, 49152, how)`: the block's mapping succeeds, the growth by `mremap` and `realloc`'s new mapping fail, and both calls return 4 (no block; the old one live with its bytes). The model's line (`Eval.enomemLine`: the same call under the failure oracle `fails := fun i _ => i ≥ 1`, over the 0.17.1 and the 0.16.0 translation) and the native runs' (stock Zig 0.16.0 and 0.17.1, `ReleaseSafe`, x86_64-linux) equal the `page_grow_enomem` line of `expected-linux.txt` ([alloc-page.md](docs/alloc-page.md#growth-by-remap-and-realloc-m02)). macOS has no `mremap` and does not enforce `RLIMIT_AS`: no line there.

Closed since: the fixed buffer on Zig 0.17.1 is exported and translated, and the same `FAllocSpec` proof holds over its translation ([PR823](https://github.com/riventic/air2lean/pull/823)). The fixed buffer's growth by `remap` and `realloc` is compared natively (`tests/roadmap/alloc-translated`, `fba_grow`, [README](tests/roadmap/alloc-translated/README.md#the-fixed-buffer)): in place as the last allocation, a shrink, behind another block (`remap` fails with both blocks unchanged; `realloc` moves, copies and leaves the old block's bytes used up), past the buffer (both fail, the old block unchanged); each result includes the buffer's end index. The model's line (the 0.16.0 and the 0.17.1 translation, x86_64-linux and aarch64-macos) equals the native runs' (stock Zig 0.16.0 and 0.17.1, `ReleaseSafe`, aarch64-macos and x86_64-linux).

Beyond the sentence, recorded elsewhere: `realloc` of other element types and sentinel slices (the wrapper contracts of [alloc-spec.md](docs/alloc-spec.md), M04), custom allocator identity (M01) and address reuse (M05).
