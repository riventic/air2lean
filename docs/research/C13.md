# C13 — Termination, fairness and starvation

Status: research ([ROADMAP](../../ROADMAP.md),
[acceptance](../../remaining-acceptance.md#c13--termination-fairness-and-starvation)).

## Question

The acceptance asks for explicit scheduler and memory-progress assumptions, total-correctness rules,
variants and liveness reasoning; deadlock, starvation and divergence are distinct properties; one
task-completion or shutdown theorem is proved under stated assumptions; bounded fuel exhaustion is
never presented as termination evidence. What does a termination proof of concurrent code that
waits (mutex, `WaitGroup`, `Event`, `Condition`, futex retry loops, weak-CAS loops) rest on, which
premise states it, and how does it live in the one proof framework (credits in `Res`, `FTTriple`/
`CTriple`, `fsym`) without a new judgment or tactic?

## What exists

- **Credits** (merged on `codex/alloc-arena-proof`, `docs/sep-full-state.md` §Credits and room):
  `£ n` is a non-mintable ghost component of `Res`; the adequacy theorem keeps the authority
  `●N` in the frame and gives `main` `£ N`. `docs/sep-threads.md` §Termination plans `WPT`, the total
  variant of the concurrent judgment: each sync node consumes `£ 1` from the running thread's part; a
  spawn of a target with budget `k` moves `£ k` to the child and costs `£ 2`; `Proto.returnsWithin`
  re-proves the rejected #178 potential argument (`bsum`, `settle_budget`, `fork_blocks`,
  `join_blocks`) with the budget read from the resources. A futex wait and a deferred task have **no
  rule** (fail closed). This gives `ReturnsWithin B` (uniform in the oracle), with no fairness.
- **Deadlock** is a model error (`Zig.Error.deadlock`: no thread can go on and one has not ended),
  and strict-safety theorems (`Proto.run_safe`) exclude it. It is a safety property.
- **Divergence** of a thread alone (a loop with no exit) is excluded by the sequential total
  rules (`FTotalTriple`, loop variants) and, in the concurrent judgment, by credits.
- **Starvation** has only negative results today: `idle_starves`
  (`tests/roadmap/idle-loops/IdleLoop/Theorems.lean`: an oracle that always runs the spinning
  worker; no fuel gives a result) and `progress_needs_premise`. The one positive progress result,
  `idle_progress`, assumes `Cooperative o` ([THR-09](../premises.md#thr-09)): from some index on
  the oracle always takes option 0.
- `Conc/Total.lean` has the result interfaces `ReturnsWithin B`, `EventuallyReturns`,
  `EventuallyReturnsUnder Fair` with `under_false` (vacuity guard) and `discharge`.
  [THR-07](../premises.md#thr-07) says yield/spin hints are not fairness.

**Native observation** (`docs/research/c13-probe/probe.c`, recorded outputs next to it): the
algorithm of `Io.Mutex` over the raw OS futex, two threads for 2 s, one relocking at once (the
hog) and one doing about 2 us of work outside the lock (the victim).

| Platform | hog acquisitions | victim acquisitions | victim worst wait |
|---|---|---|---|
| aarch64-macos (`__ulock`) | 16.3 M | 0.70 M | 5.6 ms |
| x86_64-linux (Docker/Rosetta, `futex`) | 15.5 M | 0.68 M | 1.8 ms |

The hog takes 96% of the acquisitions: there is no fairness. The victim is not starved here
(unbounded starvation is not observable in 2 s, so the probe shows imbalance, not starvation).
The second observation: a futex wait with an unchanged word and no wake returns after one signal
(`EINTR` on both platforms), i.e. a spurious return is a real outcome, not only a model option.

## Properties, kept apart

| Property | Meaning | Where it is proved |
|---|---|---|
| Deadlock freedom | some thread can always go on until all ended | strict safety; no fairness |
| Divergence freedom (per thread) | a thread run alone ends | sequential total rules, credits |
| System progress (task completion, shutdown) | the whole run ends: every thread finishes | credits + **weak fairness of ready threads** (below) |
| Starvation freedom of a given thread | that thread ends although others keep working | needs strong fairness of wake choice (SCH-02), which no target promises; **not claimed** |

## Why a fairness premise is unavoidable

A waiter's retry loop has no bound in its own turns. Counterexample family: the oracle that never picks the thread that must make progress. For a
spin it is the kernel-checked `idle_starves`; for the futex mutex it is an argument here, not yet
a checked theorem (acceptance item 3). For a futex wait the model's spurious return (THR-05, option 1 of every
sleeping wait) turns the wait into a spin: a mutex waiter (`while swap(2) != 0 futexWait`) whose
every wait returns spuriously stays ready, and an oracle that always prefers it starves the holder,
so `lock` never returns although no deadlock exists. Spurious returns are native (above), so this
is a legal native behaviour of an adversarial scheduler, and it cannot be excluded by credits:
credits count a thread's own turns, and the waiter's turns do not end. A premise on the
**scheduler** is needed; nothing is needed on the futex beyond what the model has, because a spurious
return only makes the waiter ready more often.

## Options

1. **A per-client schedule premise (status quo, THR-09 `Cooperative`).** Cheap, but client-specific
   and wrong in general: "always the lowest ready thread" starves a higher thread forever when the
   lowest one spins (so it cannot state progress for `main` waiting on a child). It also
   does not generalize to retry loops or `WaitGroup`. Rejected as the mechanism; kept as a legacy
   special case until `idle_progress` is re-proved under option 3.
2. **Bounded bypass `K` (a ready thread is skipped at most `K` turns).** Gives a uniform
   `ReturnsWithin B*(1+K*n)` through credits (a helper's step pays for the `K*n` waiter turns it
   allows). But turns are not time: no OS promises a `K`, and a premise with a free parameter is
   either false or a tuning knob. Not the base premise; it is a corollary if a target ever documents
   one.
3. **Weak fairness of ready threads + wait tokens (recommended).** One scheduler premise (a thread
   that is ready from some turn on is eventually picked). Waiting iterations are justified in the
   program logic by a ghost token naming a helper that is ready and has credits; credits are spent
   only by progress steps; the adequacy theorem applies the premise once. Below.
4. **TaDA Live obligations (D'Osualdo, Sutherland, Farzan, Gardner, TOPLAS 2021).** Obligations,
   wait levels and fairness for blocking code, compositional and complete. It needs a full
   obligation and level theory per resource. We take the two ideas that fit credits (a wait token with a level to
   exclude wait cycles, an owed-action resource) and not its assertion language.
5. **LiLi (Liang and Feng, POPL 2016) / HMRS-style lock-freedom credits (Hoffmann et al., LICS
   2013).** LiLi states progress of concurrent objects under fair scheduling; its "will-complete"
   assertions are the object-level reading of option 3. HMRS charges a failed CAS to the thread whose
   successful CAS interfered, with credits transferred on interference. That needs **no fairness** for
   lock-free loops, and it is the right rule for CAS retry (the credit transfer is a library lemma on
   the existing credits). It does not cover spurious weak-CAS failure (below).
6. **Iris later credits (Spies et al., ICFP 2022).** They remove a `▷` in step-indexed logics. The
   full-state logic is not step-indexed (adequacy is an induction on fuel), so there is nothing
   to eliminate. Not used. Time credits (Mevel, Jourdan, Pottier, ESOP 2019) are exactly our `£ n`;
   fuel-based fair-termination logics (the Trillium line in Iris) match option 3's structure but
   are tied to its machinery. We reuse the shape, not the logic.

## Recommendation: option 3, with HMRS credit transfer for CAS loops

**Premises** (new IDs, to be added to `docs/premises.md` and `assurance/premises.json` with the
implementation; kind `environment` except where noted):

- **SCH-01, weak fairness of the scheduler.** Over the run's trace, a thread that is ready at every
  turn from some turn on is picked at some later turn. It is a hypothesis of a progress theorem, never a
  property of the model: every oracle stays a legal schedule and the safety theorems quantify over
  all of them. It supersedes THR-09 (whose `Cooperative` implies nothing about fairness). Native
  reading: default time-sharing classes; false for `SCHED_FIFO`/real-time and for SIGSTOP-ed
  threads. It says nothing about time.
- **No futex fairness premise.** Spurious returns and the arbitrary wake choice (THR-05, OSF-01,
  OSF-02) stay adversarial: every liveness proof holds for every spurious pattern and every wake
  choice. That is sound for what is claimed (system progress), because a woken or spuriously
  returned waiter is ready, and weak fairness is about ready threads. A sleeping waiter is not ready
  and needs a wake, which the protocol invariant gives (an unlock that saw `contended` wakes one; the
  woken waiter relocks with `contended`, so the chain continues): this is a safety-style invariant
  the mutex/`WaitGroup` proof already needs.
- **SCH-02 (not assumed): strong fairness of wake choice.** Needed for starvation freedom of one
  waiter; no target promises it. Claims say "all threads finish when every thread does finitely many
  acquisitions", never "every waiter acquires".
- **ORD-07, weak-CAS forward progress** (kind `trusted`): a `cmpxchg_weak` loop on one location
  does not fail spuriously forever without a write by another thread (the architecture's
  load-/store-exclusive forward-progress rule; ORD-04 keeps the spurious failure itself). Stated as a
  condition on the oracle's spurious choices, in the same fairness predicate as SCH-01. This needs a
  cited architecture clause (ARMv8 exclusives; x86 has no weak CAS) before it is admitted.
- Timed waits (`Io.futexWaitTimeout`, deadline futexes) additionally need that clocks advance
  (OSK-01 `Env.clock`, unbounded); they stay outside until a clock-progress premise exists.

**The fairness predicate.** It is on the run's trace, not on `o`: the oracle picks index
`o i % ready.size` into a ready array whose order depends on the state, so "o eventually picks
thread t" is not expressible on `o` alone. `WeakFair` quantifies over the state sequence of
`Sched.go`: `∀ t i, (∀ j ≥ i, t ∈ ready (s j)) → ∃ j ≥ i, pick (s j) = t`. It slots into the
existing interface as `EventuallyReturnsUnder (WeakFair env dispatch main m)`, whose `Fair` argument is
an arbitrary predicate on the oracle, so `Total.lean` needs no new interface. Open point to settle
first: `Sched.go` threads `fuel` through each thread's run depth (`turnTrace … fuel`), so the state
sequence is fuel-dependent; phase 1 must show it is prefix-stable in the fuel (or define fairness
as a property of every fuel-`F` prefix).

**Proof rules (no new judgment).** `WPT` stays the total variant of the concurrent judgment; the
changes are resources and `@[fsym]` rules:

1. *Progress steps* consume `£ 1`, as designed. A progress step is any step that is not a
   wait iteration.
2. *A wait token* `Wait h ℓ`, a ghost fragment in `Res` like `£ n` (non-mintable through the same
   authoritative-supply pattern; the authority is held by the invariant): it states that this thread
   is retrying on behalf of thread `h` at level `ℓ`. The rule for a futex wait / retry-loop
   iteration costs **no** credit if the precondition holds a `Wait h ℓ` and the protocol invariant
   gives (a) `h` is not finished and has `£ ≥ 1`, (b) `h`'s own state is a progress step or a wait at
   a level `< ℓ` (levels exclude wait cycles; deadlock freedom is already a strict-safety
   consequence, the level is the quantitative form). The token is created by the lock/event
   invariant (the acquirer holds `£ k` for its critical section, the unlock hands the next
   waiter its token), so module proofs supply only that invariant.
3. *Spurious wakeups* are covered by the same rule: the retry loop re-establishes the token on
   every iteration; the primitive wait has no rule of its own. `fsym` charges nothing to the
   waiter and obliges nothing about the oracle's spurious choice.
4. *CAS loops* (lock-free, no wait token): a failed `cmpxchg` caused by interference consumes a credit
   transferred from the interfering success (HMRS), an `@[fsym]` rule over the CAS success
   postcondition. A spuriously failing weak CAS under ORD-07 is a wait iteration whose helper is
   "the architecture", i.e. charged against the fairness predicate, not against a thread.
5. *Adequacy* (library theorem, the one place the premise enters): from `WeakFair`, no run has a suffix
   of only token steps. Take a suffix with no credit consumed; every ready thread is in a wait
   iteration; the one with the least level has a helper of lower level that is ready and has a credit
   (or is a progress thread); the helper is ready from then on, so by `WeakFair` it is picked and
   consumes a credit, contradiction with the suffix. Hence the potential (sum of credits, plus the
   authority `●N`) strictly falls infinitely often, and it is a `Nat`. Result:
   `EventuallyReturnsUnder (WeakFair …)`; the bound depends on the oracle (no uniform `ReturnsWithin`
   exists once waiting is admitted: `stuck_not_returnsWithin`-style schedules delay the helper
   arbitrarily).
6. *Spawn/join* are unchanged. `join` is a wait on the child (token `Wait child ℓ`); `Group.await`
   likewise; a `WaitGroup.wait` waits on the last `finish`, whose owner holds the token source.

**Reuse and what is not rebuilt.** The #178 counting lemmas (`bsum`, `settle_budget`,
`fork_blocks`, `join_blocks`) move into the library as the credit potential; `Cooperative`-based
`idle_progress` is re-proved as the first client under SCH-01 (the worker waits on `main`, which
holds `£ 3`; `main` is ready, so it is picked); `Proofs/Threadsync` mutex/`WaitGroup` supply
invariants that already exist for safety plus credits per critical section.

**Fail-closed until implemented.**

- Keep `opCost = none` for futex wait and deferred tasks in the `WPT` rules: no total
  concurrent theorem over code that waits can be stated.
- Any theorem or claim using `EventuallyReturnsUnder` with a non-trivial `Fair` must list the
  premise that defines it. Today that is THR-09 for `Cooperative`; `scripts/premises.py` should
  tie the token `EventuallyReturnsUnder` to a fairness premise (SCH-01 or THR-09) and fail when a
  theorem mentions it without one. `under_false` already guards vacuity.
- `claims.py` must not render a bounded-fuel `some`/`none` run, a `countdown`-style singleton
  result or a `ReturnsWithin` over a client without waiting as "termination of concurrent code"
  unless the closure has no sync op that blocks; the acceptance wording "bounded fuel exhaustion is
  not evidence" is already true of `Sched.run` (out of fuel is `none`).
- A translated program that contains a futex wait and a termination goal is rejected by the
  total translation mode with a diagnostic naming SCH-01 until phase 2 lands.

## Acceptance evidence

1. A task-completion theorem for a translated client with waiting: `main` spawns workers that
   increment a mutex-protected counter (`threadsync.mutexCounter` over the translated std mutex) and
   `WaitGroup`/join, and returns the sum; `EventuallyReturnsUnder (WeakFair …)` with SCH-01 listed
   in its premises, proved by `fsym` with only the mutex invariant (no per-module tactic).
2. `idle_progress` re-proved from SCH-01, and `Cooperative` shown not to imply `WeakFair`.
3. A kernel-checked counterexample that SCH-01 is needed for the mutex client (an oracle that
   always takes the spurious option and prefers the waiter; the analogue of `idle_starves`) and
   one that spurious returns do not break the theorem.
4. Deadlock, starvation and divergence each have a named example; a premise-check test shows a
   theorem with `Fair` and no fairness premise is rejected.
5. The probe recorded here (barging, `EINTR`) is cited from the premise text.

## Decisions for the maintainer

1. Approve SCH-01 (weak fairness) as the one scheduler progress premise, replacing THR-09, and
   the decision not to assume futex fairness or SCH-02 (no starvation-freedom claims).
2. Whether results under SCH-01 are `EventuallyReturnsUnder` only (oracle-dependent bound) or
   whether a bounded-bypass `K` corollary (option 2) is wanted for cost statements.
3. Whether wait tokens belong in `Res` (a second counted ghost cell next to credits) or in the
   protocol invariant's own ghost state; the recommendation is `Res`, so `fsym` rules can see them.
