# V01 and V02 — Formal AIR semantics; normalization and emission preservation

Status: research; both rows stay `research`
([ROADMAP](../../ROADMAP.md), [V01](../../remaining-acceptance.md#v01--formal-air-semantics),
[V02](../../remaining-acceptance.md#v02--normalization-and-emission-preservation)). The design and
the implemented slice are in [air-semantics.md](../air-semantics.md); this page decides how to
reach the full acceptance, and what the pointer-poison semantics is.

## Question

V01 asks for a formal semantics of every AIR operation the checker admits, with explicit premises
for the modelled external operations. V02 asks that each accepted translation comes with a
checked relation to that semantics, for the normalizing rewrites (read-only-copy forwarding,
reference rewrites), control flow, escaping locals, indirect calls and the generated encodings.
Today `Air2Lean/Sem.lean` (1,692 lines, a definitional interpreter over the decoded `Func` and
`Zig.MemM`) and the 13 committed `AirCert.lean` files certify 23 of 124 functions, and
`Canon.lean` is outside the certificates. How does this scale to the whole language, and what
does a pointer formed outside its allocation mean?

## Where the slice stands (measured)

* **Operations.** The checked-in op table (`coverage/op-table/op-table.json`) decodes 176 of 199
  named tags to one of 103 `Op` constructors. `Sem.lean` has a match arm for 49 of those
  constructors (by name: partial type coverage is not counted out) and so about 89 of the 176
  decoded tags. Missing: floats (`divFloat`, `sqrt`, `libm`, `mulAdd`, `floatConv`, `floatFromInt`,
  `intFromFloat`, `neg`, `abs`, `floatRound`), vectors (`splat`, `select`, `shuffle`, `reduce`,
  `vectorStoreElem`), aggregates and unions (`structFieldVal`, `aggregateInit`, `unionTag`,
  `unionInit`, `setUnionTag`), pointer-form optionals and error unions (`isNullPtr`,
  `optPayloadPtr`, `isErrPtr`, `errPayloadPtr`, `errCodePtr`, `tryPtr`), bulk memory (`memset`,
  `memcpy`), the atomics (`atomicLoad`, `atomicStore`, `atomicRmw`, `cmpxchg`), `asm`,
  `arrayElemVal`, `arrayToSlice`, `fieldParentPtr`, bit counting and permutation, the overflow
  builtins, `tagName`, `errorName`, and the loop/switch dispatch forms.
* **Versions.** The coverage inventories (L01: 207, 212, 214, 224 tags for 0.14.1, 0.15.2,
  0.16.0, 0.17.0) classify every tag, but their `semantics` and `proofs` columns are
  `symbol-index-only` for all 224 tags of 0.17.0: they say that a symbol mentions the tag, not
  that the semantics interprets it. One `Sem` serves all versions, because `Canon.versionTags`
  renames 0.17.0 tags to the 0.16.0 vocabulary and `Normalize` builds one `Func`; 0.17.0 has 35
  `emitted-unfixtured` tags (20.6% of its 170 emitted tags), so the 0.17.0 vocabulary is the one with
  least executable evidence.
* **Functions.** 23 of 124 (18.5%) in the 13 certified examples (the exclusion table in
  air-semantics.md: 55 on a non-scalar parameter, 25 concurrent, 7 on the return type, 5 on an
  instruction). The exclusions are by type family (aggregates, enums, floats, vectors) and by
  concurrency, not by operation: the missing *value kinds* gate most functions.
* **Canonicalization.** `Canon.lean` has 10 steps on `main` (`versionTags`, `argRanks`,
  `errTraces`, `dropDeadAllocPlaceholders`, `dropDeadRuntimeLanePtrs`, `forwardReadOnlyCopies`,
  `itemReads`, `dropTrueChecks`, `sinkPastChecks`, then `renumber`). None is proved. The parked
  `codex/canon-cert` branch (read, not continued) decoded `sinkPastChecks` onto the `Func`
  (`Air/Sink.lean`), gave `Sem` a `poison` value for out-of-allocation pointer formation, and has
  the proof plan for a general sink theorem unfinished (reader/commutation lemmas `rd_comm`,
  `Keeps`, `Commute`, `Bubble`).
* **Framework fit.** `Sem` already runs in `Zig.MemM`, the monad `FTriple` and `FTotalTriple` are
  stated over (`ZigLean/Sep/Full/Triple.lean`: `∀ m r rF, Holds m r rF → P r → … (c.run m).run`).
  A certificate `f_step` is an equation of `MemM` actions, so it transports any `FTriple` between
  the generated definition and the AIR meaning without a new judgment (see Recommendation).

## What Zig and LLVM do with a pointer outside its allocation

This is the open decision from the Canon track: `Zig.ptrProject` throws `.illegal` at formation
(MM-3, `docs/build-modes.md`), so the model's order differs from the native program where Sema
forms `ptr_add(p, a)` before the check that guards `a`; `sinkPastChecks` repairs the order.

Evidence, per layer.

1. **Zig 0.17.0 LLVM backend** (`src/codegen/llvm/FuncGen.zig`, pinned source): `ptraddConst` and
   `ptraddScaled` emit `wip.gep(.inbounds, …)` (lines 7963 and 7973); `airPtrAdd` and
   `airPtrSub` go through them; `fieldPtr`, `airPtrElemPtr` and `airSliceElemPtr` use the same
   helpers (checked). `grep -rn freeze src/codegen/llvm*` finds nothing:
   Zig never freezes a pointer or a comparison. The 0.14.1, 0.15.2 and 0.16.0 backends use
   `getelementptr inbounds` as well (the existing MM-3 note).
2. **LLVM LangRef.** A `getelementptr inbounds` whose base or result is not within the same
   allocated object (one past the end allowed) yields poison. A poison operand makes `icmp` and
   `ptrtoint`/`gep` results poison; the undefined behaviour is *using* poison as a branch or
   switch condition, as an address of a memory access, as an argument or result carrying
   `noundef` (Zig puts `noundef` on integer results and on `byref_mut` parameters), or in another
   instruction with a side effect. Poison is not undefined behaviour on its own, which is why the
   native program panics at the preceding check.
3. **Native observation** (recorded, `tests/roadmap/air-semantics/poison/`, Zig 0.14.1 and 0.15.2,
   `-OReleaseSafe -fllvm`; 0.17.0's stock compiler is not available here, the AIR-only compilers
   in the CAS cannot emit LLVM IR): `p + n == q` and `@intFromPtr(p + n) < @intFromPtr(q)` compile
   to `%3 = getelementptr inbounds i8, ptr %0, i64 %2` then `icmp eq|ult ptr %3, %1` with no
   `freeze`, and the function returns the comparison (`zeroext i1`, no `noundef`). So the compare
   of poison is a poison result that the program may return and use later; it is undefined only
   where it is branched on. `if (@intFromPtr(p + n) == 0)` folds to the constant `ret i8 2`: the
   optimizer already uses "an inbounds gep of a non-null base is non-null", a fact that holds
   only because the out-of-bounds case is poison.
4. **Self-hosted x86_64 backend** (Debug default on Linux, 0.17.0 `codegen/x86_64/CodeGen.zig`
   line 43348): `ptr_add` is a `lea`. No poison; a wrapped or out-of-allocation pointer is a
   plain integer there. Poison semantics is therefore a *superset* of the behaviours of the
   backends: a model that calls the use illegal is sound for both.

Conclusion: native behaviour of a pointer formed out of its allocation is "poison, which is
harmless until used and then unconstrained", with comparisons and `@intFromPtr` of poison also
poison. The model's order requirement (check first, then the formation matters) is a *use* rule,
not a formation rule.

## Options

### Pointer poison (the decision asked for)

1. **Eager (today): `ptrProject` is `.illegal` at formation, plus `sinkPastChecks`.** Sound
   (`.illegal` over-approximates every native behaviour) but order-sensitive: the model is only
   adequate if each formation sits after the checks that guard it. That needs a reordering pass
   in the trusted base (Canon rewrite 7, whose proof is a long commutation argument over every
   instruction that may sit between formation and use; the canon-cert plan), restricted to a
   whitelist of effect-free instructions (a call in between could resize the base block, so the
   pass is only correct under `noResize`). Formations that are never used at all (for example a
   loop's final `p + 1` past the one-past-the-end pointer) are `.illegal` although native code is
   fine: a completeness loss, not a soundness one.
2. **Lazy pointers, compare of poison illegal (recommended).** Add a poison state to `Zig.Ptr`;
   `ptrProject` never throws, it returns a poison pointer when the result leaves
   `[0, size]` of the base's block (or the base is poison, or has no block and the offset is not
   0). Data movement keeps poison (store/load of the pointer bytes via `ptrFrag`, copy,
   `slice`/`slice_ptr`, further `ptr_add`/field/element projections, `bitcast`); every
   *inspection* is illegal: access (`load`, `store`, `memcpy`, atomics, `free`), `ptrAddr`,
   `ptrEqAddr`/`ptrLt`/`ptrLe`, `@intFromPtr`, a pointer argument of an external model. So
   "formation of poison, then a bounds check that panics" is the panic, as natively, with no
   reordering. It matches LLVM exactly for the pointer register class and over-approximates in
   one place: a comparison of a poison pointer is `.illegal` immediately, where LLVM yields a
   poison boolean that is undefined only if branched on (or returned under `noundef`). That is
   the documented conservative over-approximation (CLAUDE.md "Fail closed"). It matters only for
   a poison comparison whose result is dropped or only returned, which no corpus function does;
   a lint (below) counts it.
3. **Full poison propagation: `Value.poison` for every scalar** (LLVM's model). Exact, but every
   `Zig.add`, branch and call in generated code, and every `FTriple` rule, gains a poison case;
   the whole proof corpus is re-walked for a gain confined to a dead comparison. Rejected
   unless the lint finds a real function that needs it.
4. **Nondeterministic result of a poison comparison** (`MemM` choice). The model is
   deterministic by design (placement is the only choice, SEM-07). Rejected; `.illegal` is the
   only value that every native outcome refines.

Why 2 beats 1: it moves the rule from a reordering of the program (a transformation whose proof
must see every instruction kind and every callee) to a property of one data type, which is also
what `Sem` already does (the canon-cert `Value.poison`), so the semantics and the generated code
share the rule instead of the certificate having to bridge them. It removes a rewrite from
`Canon.lean`, deletes `Air/Sink.lean`, and makes `sinkPastChecks`'s `noResize` exclusion
unnecessary (a poison pointer stays poison when the base block grows, which is again LLVM's
rule: poison is a value property, decided at formation). Cost: `Zig.Ptr` is used in 120 files;
the field is additive (`block : Option BlockId`, `off : Int`, plus a `poison : Bool`, default
false, or an inductive provenance `block b | none | poison`); `ptrProject`'s type does not
change, so the 7 `Emit.lean` sites and generated code do not change; lemmas that say
"`ptrProject` throws `.illegal`" (`Proofs/Slices` `subZ_oob`, `Proofs/Atomics/Stack`) are
re-walked to say the later use throws. `fsym` needs a rule `ptrProject` of an in-bounds
pointer = pure, which is the same side condition as now.

### Scaling the semantics (V01)

1. **One definitional interpreter, extended tag by tag (current, recommended).** Every new
   operation is a match arm mapping to the ZigLean primitive the generated code uses. The
   arms are the meaning (SEM-06), so they stay few lines each and reviewable. Needs per value kind
   a `Value` constructor and an `Enc` bridge.
2. **A relational semantics (small-step).** Needed only for concurrency (`ConcM`); not for the
   sequential rest. Add later as the interpreter's `ConcM` instance, not instead.
3. **Generate the interpreter from the op table.** The table's rows (effect, emit route) do not
   carry the meaning; generating would hide the meaning inside a generator. Rejected. What
   the table *should* feed is a coverage lint (Stage 0).
4. **Per-version semantics.** Rejected: the rename layer (`versionTags`) is the one place that
   differs per version and is decoder specification (checked by op-effects tests), so the
   interpreter stays version-free; a new Zig version adds rename rows, not semantics.

### Scaling the preservation proofs (V02)

1. **Verified emitter.** Prove `Emit.lean` (about 4,000 lines of string generation with several
   strategies per construct). Rejected: it re-implements the emitter.
2. **Per-function translation validation by family, one generic proof script (current,
   recommended).** The generator emits the same script per function; a function the script cannot
   normalize fails `lake build`. Scales by adding value kinds, not by per-function work.
3. **A general simulation theorem** for the structural part (block/branch/loop/call), so
   that a certificate only has to discharge straight-line leaves. The loop commutation lemma
   (`loop_comm`) is the first instance; the others are generated per function today. Worth doing
   once the certificate size becomes the cost (Pointers: 517 lines for 6 functions), not before.
4. **For `Canon`: general theorems per local rewrite, validation per instance for the rest.**
   `renumber` (α-equivalence over `Sem`), `dropDeadAllocPlaceholders` and
   `dropDeadRuntimeLanePtrs` (dead pure code), `itemReads` (fusion lemma over
   `ptrProject`/`load`) and `dropTrueChecks` (a check whose condition is provably true is a
   no-op) are general. `versionTags` and `argRanks` are decoder specification, and stay
   trusted with op-effects tests. `forwardReadOnlyCopies` is the hard one.

### `forwardReadOnlyCopies` (the placement obstruction)

The raw function allocates a block for the read-only copy; the canonical one does not.
air-semantics.md §Locals proves that a memory extension relation is not preserved by later
allocations under SEM-07 (`PlacementCounterexample.lean`), and a prophecy placement is blocked
by the fallback `Mem.top`, which lies past every block, dead or alive, so a placement shifted by
one block id cannot compensate. Two ways out:

* **A per-run prophecy theorem** ("for every `σ` there is a `σ'` such that …", with `σ'`
  defined from the run): not a per-function normalization but a whole-program simulation, and
  `Mem.top` makes `σ'` depend on every later allocation. Large and fragile.
* **Extend register cells to unobservable-address locals (recommended).** The copy's address is
  never observed: its only uses are `bitcast` to const pointer, projections with constant
  offsets and `load`s (that is exactly the shape the rewrite accepts). Treat such an `alloc` in
  `Sem` as a frame cell holding a *structured* value (projection reads a field of the stored
  value), the same decision already taken for register locals and justified in §Locals (an
  unobservable block can only change other blocks' addresses, and every theorem holds for every
  placement). Then the raw and canonical functions both allocate no block; the rewrite is a
  functional equivalence (the load of a projection of a stored value is the projection of the
  value), proved once. The semantics' decision is part of SEM-06 and is syntactic (decided on the
  decoded AIR, like `regAlloc`). It also covers `dropDeadAllocPlaceholders` (a dead alloc is a
  cell nobody reads).

## Recommendation

**Keep the one definitional interpreter and the per-family generated certificates; scale them by
value kind; take `Canon` out of the trusted base with general theorems for the local rewrites;
make pointer poison lazy and delete `sinkPastChecks`.**

Concretely:

1. *Poison-on-use for pointers*, option 2 above: `Zig.Ptr` gains a poison state; `ptrProject`
   returns it instead of throwing; all inspections are `.illegal`; a comparison of a poison
   pointer is `.illegal` (documented over-approximation of LLVM's poison boolean; evidence above).
   `Sem` and the generated code share it. Rewrite 7 and `Air/Sink.lean` are deleted.
2. *Raw-AIR certificates*, the form fixed in air-semantics.md §Canonicalization: the certificate
   embeds the decoded raw AIR (`decode` of the golden JSON, round-trip checked), and a
   per-rewrite theorem `execFunc o (R r) args ≈ execFunc o r args` is in the library, so a
   certificate lists which rewrites fired (`canonicalizeTraced`) and fails closed
   ("trusted-Canon: <passes>") for an uncertified one.
3. *Unobservable-address locals as cells* for `forwardReadOnlyCopies`.
4. *A coverage lint* in the op-table test: every `Op` constructor is either interpreted by `Sem`
   (a registered arm) or on an explicit `Sem.outsideFragment` list with a reason; the inventory's
   `semantics` column then says `interpreted | outside-fragment` instead of `symbol-index-only`
   (L01 follow-up, whole-tree file, generated on the merged tree).

### How certificates relate to the full-state logic

There is one framework (CLAUDE.md): `FTriple`/`FTotalTriple`/`CTriple` walked by `fsym`. The
semantics already targets the same `MemM`, so:

* Proofs stay about the generated definition `Gen.f`, which `fsym` walks. A certificate
  `(execFunc o air_f args).run m = ((enc <$> Gen.f args).run m)` for all `m` is the bridge: a
  library lemma `FTriple.congr` (`∀ m, c.run m = c'.run m → (FTriple P c Q ↔ FTriple P c' Q)`,
  with `Q` precomposed with `enc`) gives the triple about the AIR meaning from the proved triple
  about `Gen.f`, and conversely. That is a lemma, not a judgment, tactic or template. A theorem
  about `Gen.f` then *is* a theorem about the AIR of that function, for a certified function.
* Two traps to state in the lemma, not hide. First, `FTriple` treats `none` (the interpreter's
  `stuck`, and non-termination) as `True`, so a triple about `execFunc` alone is vacuous where
  `Sem` is stuck; the certificate's equality with the defined `Gen.f` is what rules that out, and
  `FTotalTriple` (`FReturns`) must be used for termination claims. Second, `.illegal` is a
  failure in `FTriple`, so poison-on-use's conservative compare makes proofs stricter, never
  weaker.
* No rule duplicates `fsym`: the AIR interpreter is not walked by `fsym`. If a program must be
  proved without Gen (a function the translator rejects), the stop-and-report rule applies;
  nothing here builds a second walker.
* Concurrency: `CTriple P x Q = ∀ n, FTriple P (soloRun n x) Q`. The concurrent fragment of
  `Sem` is an interpreter into `ConcM` whose `soloRun` instance is the sequential one, so the
  same bridge applies per thread body; scheduler-level claims go through the existing `CTriple`
  rules and the premise set of C12.
* Frames and memory: `Sem`'s `Frame` (register cells, stack blocks) lives next to `Mem`; the
  certificate hides it by running `execFunc` with an empty frame, so `FTriple`'s state is `Mem`
  only. Cells are not heap; no separation-logic resource is added for them.

### Fail-closed behaviour until implemented

Already in place: any operation, type or shape outside the fragment is `stuck`; the certificate
generator lists the function with its first reason; a function is never certified when its Gen is
undefined. Additions during the plan: a certified function must have all fired Canon passes
certified, or its report line says which passes are trusted (never silently "certified"); the
poison change keeps the eager rule for every pointer path not yet ported (a poison pointer
reaching an unported consumer is `.illegal`, never a guess).

### Premises

* [SEM-06](../premises.md#sem-06) (meaning of a canonical AIR function): shrinks to *raw* AIR
  when rewrites are certified; its text must name the unobservable-address-cell decision and, once
  extended, the value kinds (floats, aggregates, atomics) it covers.
* [SEM-07](../premises.md#sem-07) (block addresses are the environment's placement): unchanged;
  it is why unobservable locals can be cells.
* New, proposed `SEM-08` (next free id; `premises.json` and `premises.md` are the maintainer's to
  allocate): "a pointer formed outside its allocation is poison, and any comparison, address
  observation or access of a poison pointer is illegal behaviour". Kind: meaning. Evidence:
  this page (LLVM `gep inbounds`, no `freeze`, observed IR, `lea` in the self-hosted backend).
* Stage 5 (floats, vectors, atomics) attaches the existing float/atomic premises (FLT-*, ORD-*);
  no new one.

## Acceptance evidence that would close the rows

V01: (a) the op-table coverage lint passes with `Sem.outsideFragment` limited to operations the
checker rejects (asm without a contract, fast-math float tags, compiler-state tags) plus
concurrency while it is open; (b) every `Op` that `Check.lean` admits has an interpreter arm, a
`Sem` unit test against a native value, and an exercised row in the differential corpus
(`tests/diff`, conformance of `Sem` itself, the step 3 of "How SEM-06 shrinks"); (c) every
modeled external operation lists its premise.

V02: (a) every function of every committed example whose `Gen.lean` is its golden certifies, or
has a recorded, reviewed reason that is a rejection in `Check.lean` (not "outside the fragment");
(b) certificates are over raw AIR, and the certificate report shows zero trusted-Canon passes;
(c) the mutation test of the certificates (ten one-operator mutations today) extends to every
family; (d) indirect calls and escaping locals certify as families.

  `tests/roadmap/air-semantics/PlacementCounterexample.lean`.
* The canon-cert regression (sinking past a call that grows the base block changes the eager
  model's result) is the argument that the eager design needs `noResize`; it dissolves under
  lazy poison and is not needed.
